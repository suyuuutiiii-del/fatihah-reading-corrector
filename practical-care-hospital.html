🏥 Hospital / Clinic Help

Transport, accompaniment, practical errands and family coordination around non-emergency clinic or hospital care.

What this service entails

These are the main forms of practical help normally covered under this service.

Non-emergency accompaniment

A volunteer may accompany a person to a clinic or hospital for practical support, while medical decisions remain with the patient and qualified professionals.

Transport coordination

FARD may help arrange suitable non-emergency transport to or from an appointment or discharge.

Pharmacy and essential errands

A helper may collect already-authorised medication, food, clothing or other agreed necessities for the patient or family.

Family communication and coordination

With the patient or family’s permission, a volunteer may help coordinate who is providing transport, meals or other practical support.

Discharge support

Short-term help may be arranged for the first days after discharge, such as meals, groceries or household essentials, without replacing professional home care.

Safety and boundaries

Verify the need
A mosque, organisation or FARD moderator can confirm the local context before matching.
Protect privacy
Exact address and private contact details stay hidden until a suitable match is accepted.
Keep the role practical
This is community assistance and does not replace emergency, medical, legal or other professional services.
