🌱 Dignity & Street Welfare

A long-term welfare pathway for children and adults affected by street begging, homelessness, severe poverty or unsafe educational arrangements — helping people move from street dependence toward education, family/community care and sustainable livelihood.

Almajiri Child Welfare & Education

The aim is not to stigmatise Qur’anic pupils or teachers. It is to protect children from street begging, hunger, exploitation and lack of basic care while preserving beneficial Qur’anic education.

Child identification & safeguarding

Work through trusted schools, mosques, families and welfare organisations to identify children needing food, shelter, healthcare, protection or family tracing.

Qur’an + basic education

Support programmes that combine sound Qur’anic learning with literacy, numeracy, life skills and age-appropriate general education.

Food without begging

Organise verified meal sponsorship, school feeding and community kitchens so children do not have to beg in order to eat.

Safe accommodation

Where boarding is necessary, encourage supervised accommodation with adequate sleeping space, sanitation, safeguarding and accountable adult care.

Family tracing & reintegration

Where safe and appropriate, help reconnect children with parents or responsible guardians and support families so poverty does not simply return the child to the street.

Teacher/school strengthening

Help genuine Qur’anic schools improve funding, teaching conditions, child welfare, record-keeping and links with recognised community institutions.

Ending Street Begging Through Alternatives

Understand why the person begs

Distinguish hunger, disability, homelessness, displacement, unemployment, child exploitation and organised begging so the response addresses the real cause.

Immediate essentials

Provide verified food, clothing, hygiene support, temporary shelter referral and urgent practical care while a longer plan is prepared.

Skills & livelihood pathway

Connect able adults to apprenticeship, vocational training, tools, employment referrals or small livelihood support instead of indefinite street dependence.

Disability & elderly support

People unable to work should be connected to appropriate family, community, zakah, waqf, disability or social-welfare support rather than being treated as employment cases.

Verified zakah & sadaqah referral

Eligible people can be referred to trusted zakah or charitable support, with dignity and without public humiliation.

Follow-up, not one-day removal

Track whether education, shelter, work or family support is actually continuing. Moving someone off a street without a sustainable alternative is not a complete solution.

Community programmes Muslim Welfare can coordinate

Sponsor a Child’s Welfare
Verified recurring support for meals, education, clothing and essential care without public exposure of the child.
Adopt-a-School Support
Mosques, businesses and organisations support a verified Qur’anic school’s feeding, sanitation, classrooms and welfare needs.
Street-to-Skill
Assessment → training/apprenticeship → tools/work placement → follow-up.
Community Meal Network
Regular dignified meal points linked to caseworkers and longer-term welfare pathways rather than encouraging permanent begging locations.
Waqf for Education & Welfare
Encourage transparent, professionally governed endowments that can fund recurring education and basic welfare.
Family Stabilisation
Support vulnerable households with education, livelihood and practical care so children are less likely to be pushed onto the street.

Important safeguards

Children are not public fundraising material.
No public phone numbers, exact sleeping locations or humiliating photographs merely to attract donations.
No forced “street clearing.”
Welfare should provide safe alternatives and lawful safeguarding, not simply displace vulnerable people from one location to another.
Verify organisations and money flows.
Schools, sponsors and welfare organisations should be checked and funds accounted for transparently.
Safeguarding first.
Suspected trafficking, child exploitation, violence or immediate danger requires appropriate safeguarding authorities and qualified local organisations.
