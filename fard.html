How FARD becomes a trusted service network

1 · Need or offer
A Muslim, mosque or organisation submits a real need, or a volunteer registers a useful ability.
2 · Verification
Requests and volunteers are checked through a mosque, Islamic organisation, known community verifier or FARD moderation.
3 · Matching
FARD matches service type, area, availability, language, practical requirements and safety rules.
4 · Private hand-off
Sensitive contact and exact location details are shared only after a suitable match is accepted.
5 · Completion
The requester or verifier confirms whether the service was completed appropriately.
6 · Trust record
Repeated successful service can later support stronger verification and community trust.

FARD verification levels

Pending
Submitted but not yet checked. Not ready for matching.
Community verified
A known mosque, Islamic organisation or recognised community verifier confirms the person or request.
Skill verified
Where a task requires training, licensing or specialist competence, the claimed skill has appropriate supporting verification.
Completed service
A previous verified match was completed and confirmed. This is useful trust history, not a guarantee of future performance.

Start with one action

Trust before speed

Mosque / organisation verification
Requests can be held for local verification before volunteers are matched.
Privacy by design
This pilot asks for an area, not an exact home address. Sensitive contact details should not be posted publicly.
No direct money transfer
The pilot is for service matching, not unverified cash collection.
Admin moderation
Requests and offers enter the AL-BUSHRA moderation queue before public use.

Emergency: FARD is not an emergency service. For immediate danger, urgent medical care or crime, contact the appropriate local emergency service or a trusted local institution.
